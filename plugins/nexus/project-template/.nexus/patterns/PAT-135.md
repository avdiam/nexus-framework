# PAT-135: Evidence Artifact Proves Execution, Not Adequacy
*Version: 1.0.0 | Date: 2026-08-07 | Sprint: 107*

## Metadata
- **Type**: principle
- **Status**: active
- **Created**: 2026-08-07

## Problem

A discipline that says *"assert you did it"* can be skipped invisibly. The standard remedy is to require an artifact: *"show what you ran."* This works — a wholesale skip becomes detectable, because the artifact is either present or it is not.

But the remedy relocates the failure rather than removing it. The artifact proves that **an execution occurred**. It proves nothing about whether the **predicate covered the class**. And because a well-shaped artifact is convincing — labelled predicate, per-class table, zero-hit rows dutifully included — reviewers verify its *shape* and stop. A compliant-looking artifact whose pattern missed a punctuation variant passes review exactly as easily as a correct one, and passes it with more confidence than the prose it replaced ever earned.

The failure mode is therefore worse in one respect than what it replaced: the artifact confers credibility that the underlying work may not deserve.

## Context

- **Use When**:
  - Designing a remedy that replaces prose guidance with a required receipt, log, or recorded command
  - Authoring gate or checklist items that accept "evidence" as proof of completion
  - Reviewing an artifact that looks compliant but whose counts seem low for the class
  - Any discipline moving from self-assertion to demonstrated execution
- **Not When**:
  - The predicate is machine-generated from a schema and cannot vary between executions
  - No discretion exists in what gets matched — the tool determines the scope, not the author
  - The artifact's own correctness is independently checkable by a downstream consumer
- **Prerequisites**: The evidence artifact must embed a human-authored predicate — a pattern, a glob, a query, a sample selection — over which the author had discretion.

## Solution

When mandating an evidence artifact, **mandate a companion check on the predicate's coverage**. The artifact requirement and the coverage requirement are one remedy; shipping the first without the second buys detectability of skips at the cost of false confidence in inadequate runs.

Concretely, require that the recorded evidence make the predicate itself auditable:

- **Enumerate variants** of any name being matched — punctuation, hyphenation, spacing, casing, singular/plural. A section referred to as `Foo-Bar-Baz` in a tag and `Foo-Bar Baz` in prose is two strings, and a predicate matching one silently halves the class.
- **State the glob's breadth** explicitly, so an over-narrow scope is visible in the record rather than inferable only by rerunning it.
- **Treat a suspiciously low count as a predicate defect until disproven.** A class you expected to be contaminated coming back nearly clean is evidence about the predicate, not about the class.
- **Require an artifact on both branches.** A conditional gate item whose trigger is self-assessed is not a gate — an author who never considered the check marks it not-applicable and passes. The not-triggered branch must demand its own recorded line: an explicit not-applicable with a stated reason. A silently unconsidered check does not qualify as a suppressed one.

### Implementation

1. When selecting a remedy form, ask what the *new* form's blind spot is before adopting it. Every mechanism has one.
2. Specify the predicate's construction rules in the same place that mandates the artifact, not in a separate reference a reader may not reach.
3. Require the literal command and its per-class counts, zero-hit classes included — the zeros are where an over-narrow predicate shows.
4. Close the not-triggered branch with a required recorded line and a reason.
5. At review, spend the first attention on the predicate and only then on the results. Shape-checking the artifact is the failure mode, so invert the order deliberately.

## Rationale

- **Theoretical Basis**: Installing a verification mechanism does not remove a failure — it moves the failure to whatever the mechanism does not see. An artifact requirement moves it from *never ran* to *ran wrong*. That is a real improvement, because *ran wrong* is cheaper to catch than *never ran* — but only if someone is looking there, and a convincing artifact is precisely what stops people looking.
- **Empirical Evidence**: ISS-229 (Sprint 107) replaced prose guidance with a recorded-command requirement, specifically because prose could be skipped invisibly. The remedy worked as designed: skips became detectable. But the issue's **own first application of its own new rule** produced a compliant-looking artifact — labelled predicate, per-class table, zero-hit classes included — whose pattern matched the hyphenated section tag and missed the prose variant of the same name. One file-class was recorded at 1 hit when the true count was 8. The defect was found at Validate, not at review of the artifact, which had passed inspection on shape.
- **Design Decisions**: The remedy is a companion coverage check rather than a third layer of prose guidance. Prose had already been defeated twice in this lineage, so adding more of it would inherit the same skip path — the failure form must differ from the one that already failed.

## Anti-Patterns

- **Anti-Pattern 1 — Shape-checking the artifact**
  - **Why Wrong**: Confirming the artifact has the mandated fields verifies compliance with the *format*, not with the *intent*. A wrong predicate produces a perfectly-shaped artifact.
  - **Example**: Accepting a per-class evidence table because it lists every class and includes zero rows, without ever reading the pattern string it used.
- **Anti-Pattern 2 — Self-assessed conditional trigger**
  - **Why Wrong**: If the check only applies "when relevant" and relevance is judged by the same author, the branch that never considers it is indistinguishable from the branch that correctly ruled it out.
  - **Example**: An item worded "if this applies, record the evidence" — an author who never thought about it marks it not-applicable and passes the gate.
- **Anti-Pattern 3 — Treating a low count as good news**
  - **Why Wrong**: A clean result from a class you expected to be dirty is a hypothesis about the predicate, not a finding about the class.
  - **Example**: Recording one hit for a class known to be pervasive and moving on satisfied.
- **Anti-Pattern 4 — Answering the adequacy hole with more prose**
  - **Why Wrong**: The reason the artifact requirement was introduced is that prose guidance was skipped. A new prose layer inherits the same skip path.
  - **Example**: Adding a paragraph advising authors to "be thorough with the pattern" instead of requiring variant enumeration in the recorded output.

## Relationships
- **Requires**: An evidence-artifact discipline already in place, or being designed
- **Enhances**: PAT-121 (match-remedy-form-to-failure-cause — that one *selects* the remedy form; this one names the selected form's own failure mode, so the two operate in sequence at the same decision); PAT-112 (explicit-discipline-at-authoring-time — this is the caveat on how far an authoring-time discipline can carry); PAT-122 (honest-green — shares the structural insight that a pass signal does not prove the thing, applied to test suites rather than to evidence predicates)
- **Conflicts**: None identified
- **Alternatives**: Independent re-execution of the predicate by a reviewer — stronger, and correspondingly more expensive; appropriate where the class is high-stakes

## Consequences
- **Benefits**: Closes the adequacy hole that an artifact requirement opens; makes an over-narrow predicate visible in the record rather than only on re-execution; removes the self-assessed-trigger escape from conditional gate items.
- **Tradeoffs**: Adds authoring burden at the moment the remedy is designed, and reviewers must spend attention on the predicate rather than on the more legible results.
- **Risks**: Variant enumeration can itself be incomplete — this pattern reduces the adequacy hole, it does not close it. Where the class genuinely matters, re-execution beats inspection.

## Examples

### Before/After Comparison
**Without Pattern**: A methodology mandates a recorded command with per-class hit counts. An author records `grep -rn -E "Foo-Bar-Baz" …` across four classes and reports 0 / 1 / 0 / 1. The artifact is complete, well-formed, and passes review. The true count for one class is 8 — the prose form of the name, `Foo-Bar Baz`, was never matched, and the defect surfaces two phases later during independent validation.

**With Pattern**: The same mandate additionally requires variant enumeration. The predicate becomes `"Foo[- ]Bar[- ]Baz"`, the class reports 8, and the reviewer's first question — "what did the pattern match?" — is answerable from the record without re-running anything.

### Application Example
A checklist item requiring cross-cutting evidence is rewritten so that both branches demand output: either the per-class grep evidence, or an explicitly recorded not-applicable line with a stated reason. The wording adds that a silently unconsidered check does not qualify as a suppressed one — closing the branch where a non-consideration passed as a judgment.

## Validation
- **Success Indicator**: The recorded evidence lets a reviewer evaluate the *predicate* without re-running it, and no gate item accepts a self-assessed not-applicable without a recorded reason.
- **Edge Cases**: Where the predicate is generated rather than authored, the coverage check belongs upstream at the generator, not at each use site.

## Notes and Observations

This pattern was earned in an unusually direct way: the issue that introduced the evidence-artifact requirement produced, in its very first application of that requirement, an artifact that satisfied every stated condition and was substantively wrong. The remedy defeated itself within the same conversation that shipped it.

That origin is worth keeping attached to the pattern, because it is the strongest available argument for the pattern's own claim. If a discipline can fail on its author, in the issue that created it, while the author is paying maximum attention to exactly that discipline, then the general form of the claim — that installing a mechanism relocates the failure rather than removing it — is not a hypothetical caution. The right question when adopting any new verification mechanism is not "does this close the hole?" but "which hole does this open, and who is watching it?"
