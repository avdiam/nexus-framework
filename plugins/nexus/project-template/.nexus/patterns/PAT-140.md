# PAT-140: Probe Metric Responsiveness
*Version: 1.0.0 | Date: 2026-08-20 | Sprint: 109*

## Metadata
- **Type**: principle
- **Status**: active
- **Created**: 2026-08-20

## Problem

A derived score — a health metric, quality index, KPI, or effectiveness rating — is trusted as a measure of the thing it names, but nothing ever tests whether it can actually respond to that thing changing.

When such a score sits flat across many measurement cycles, the default reading is "stable," which reads as good news. This makes an insensitive instrument **self-concealing**: it emits well-formed, individually unimpeachable readings indefinitely, and the absence of movement is mistaken for the absence of change. The failure then compounds, because the same flat score feeds trend lines, velocity calibration, and keep/retire decisions downstream.

The problem class is not "the metric is wrong." It is that **nobody ever asked the metric a question it could fail.**

## Context

- **Use When**:
  - About to report a health score, quality index, or KPI as "stable" after several cycles of little or no movement
  - About to consume a score for a trend line, velocity calibration, or a keep/retire decision
  - A genuine correctness fix or known-good remediation just moved a score in the unexpected direction
  - Reviewing a scoring formula that claims to implement a documented ladder, rubric, or maturity spec
- **Not When**:
  - The metric has an intended floor or ceiling and is legitimately saturated there
  - The doubt concerns a *single* reading's fixture or oracle — audit that instrument with PAT-138 instead
  - Multiple independent sources of the same metric already disagree — compare the sources (PAT-097) rather than probing one
- **Prerequisites**: the ability to apply a change known to be a genuine improvement to the measured subject, and to re-measure without altering the formula in between

## Solution

**Probe the metric before trusting it.** Apply a change you already know to be a real improvement to the measured subject, re-measure, and require a delta in the expected **direction** and of a defensible **magnitude**. A metric that cannot move under correct maintenance is measuring something other than what it names.

Three failure signatures, each with a distinct diagnosis:

1. **No movement under real improvement** → the metric is *insensitive*. Its dominant terms are unrelated to the work being measured.
2. **Movement in the wrong direction after a correctness fix** → the formula's *weights contradict the spec they claim to implement*. This is the sharpest signature, because it is unambiguous: correctness cannot legitimately cost points.
3. **The score is dominated by a term that grows regardless of the work** — age, count, staleness of a permanently-skipped item → it is measuring *elapsed time*, not health.

Until the probe passes, **quarantine the score**: it may still be reported, but it must not feed trend, velocity, or decisions, and the quarantine must be written into the artifact rather than delivered verbally.

**Change the formula outside the measurement, never during it.** Patching weights mid-cycle makes the initial and final scores incomparable and destroys the very evidence that motivated the fix.

### Implementation

1. **Name the subject and the improvement** — state, before measuring, what you did and which direction the score should move.
2. **Record the before value**, and confirm it was *measured*, not inherited, estimated, or carried forward.
3. **Apply the known-good change.**
4. **Re-measure** with the formula untouched.
5. **Classify** against the three signatures above.
6. **Rule**: pass → the score is usable. Fail → log the defect against the formula's owner, quarantine the score, and do not launder the failure into the next trend line.

### Quick Reference

| Observation | Diagnosis | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Δ ≈ 0 after genuine improvement | Insensitive — dominant terms unrelated to the work | Quarantine; identify what the terms actually track |
| Δ < 0 after a correctness fix | Weights invert the spec's own ladder | Fix formula (outside the measurement); log as a defect |
| Δ driven by age/count/staleness alone | Measuring elapsed time, not health | Exclude or freeze the term; re-baseline |
| Before value was an estimate, not a measurement | Baseline is fabricated | Re-measure; label the delta a baseline correction, not degradation |

## Rationale

- **Theoretical Basis**: A metric is a *hypothesis* about what matters. A hypothesis with no falsification test is not knowledge. Absence of response is evidence about the instrument, not about the system — but only an active probe can distinguish the two, because both produce identical readings.
- **Empirical Evidence**: Sprint 109 (NEXUS maintenance cycle 8). The `pattern_maintenance` score moved 76.42 → 76.20 after a cycle that retired a redundant pattern, repaired 8 dangling pointers, corrected the library's only maturity misgrade, and stripped 8 duplicate-authority fields. Thirteen consecutive cycles had recorded the same non-movement as "stable at structural floor." The decisive tell was directional: promoting PAT-067 to the correct top rung **lowered** the score by 0.39, because the formula ranked `proven`=100 above `established`=80, inverting the specification's own ladder. Independently, `backup_optimization` — never once executed, 12 consecutive skips — had accrued a 10.44-point staleness penalty that measured elapsed sprints rather than backup health, and was the single largest drag on overall health for four consecutive assessments.
- **Design Decisions**: Direction is required alongside magnitude, because a small delta in the *wrong* direction is a stronger signal than a large one in the right direction. The probe uses a change already known to be good, so the test has a ground truth the metric cannot supply for itself.

## Anti-Patterns

- **Anti-Pattern 1 — Flat reads as stable**
  - **Why Wrong**: "No change" and "cannot detect change" produce the same number. Treating the flat line as a finding about the system silently converts an instrument defect into reassurance.
  - **Example**: Thirteen maintenance cycles recording "stable at structural floor" for a score that no maintenance work could ever move.

- **Anti-Pattern 2 — Fixing the metric during the measurement**
  - **Why Wrong**: Initial and final scores become incomparable, so the cycle can no longer report a delta at all — and the defect's own evidence is destroyed in the act of repairing it.
  - **Example**: Correcting inverted maturity weights mid-cycle, after the initial score was already taken under the old weights.

- **Anti-Pattern 3 — Explaining away the wrong direction**
  - **Why Wrong**: A correctness fix that lowers a score is the one signature with no benign interpretation. Rationalising it ("the drop is small," "the library is young") discards the clearest evidence the formula will ever produce.

- **Anti-Pattern 4 — Comparing against a baseline that was never measured**
  - **Why Wrong**: An honest measurement replacing an inflated estimate is arithmetically indistinguishable from degradation. Without labelling it, the correction enters the permanent record as a decline.

## Relationships

- **Requires**: none
- **Enhances**: PAT-138 (per-reading instrument audit — this pattern is its longitudinal counterpart), PAT-097 (multi-source verification)
- **Conflicts**: none
- **Alternatives**: PAT-097 — when two or more independent sources of the same metric already exist, compare them instead of probing one

## Consequences

- **Benefits**: Converts an unfalsifiable score into a testable one; prevents insensitive metrics from poisoning trend lines and velocity models; surfaces spec/formula contradictions that no individual reading can reveal.
- **Tradeoffs**: Costs a deliberate measurement cycle, and the probe is only as good as the certainty that the applied change was genuinely an improvement.
- **Risks**: A failed probe can be misread as "the metric is useless" when the correct response is usually narrower — exclude or reweight one dominant term.

## Examples

### Before/After Comparison

**Without Pattern**: A maintenance cycle retires a redundant pattern, repairs eight dangling pointers, and corrects a misgrade. The health score moves 76.42 → 76.20. The cycle is recorded as "stable," the flat number enters the trend line, and the thirteenth consecutive non-movement passes as evidence of a healthy library.

**With Pattern**: The same cycle is treated as the probe. Expected direction: up. Observed: down by 0.22, with the one pure-correctness fix accounting for −0.39 on its own. Verdict: signatures 2 and 3 both fire. The score is quarantined, the inverted weights are logged as a defect against the owning skill, and the trend line is annotated rather than extended.

### Application Example

At Sprint 109 the cycle's *own verdict on itself* depended on refusing the headline number. Read as delivered, health 94 → 94 is a null result. Decomposed, it is +2.32 and +2.68 from two cleared staleness gaps, −0.36 from permanent-skip drift, and −8.71 from replacing a fabricated baseline with the first measured value in nine sprints. On comparable terms the cycle moved 93 → 94. The aggregate number is the one that survives into every future trend line — which is precisely why the decomposition had to be written into the artifact rather than delivered verbally.

## Validation

- **Success Indicator**: A stated expected direction exists *before* the re-measurement, and the recorded verdict names one of the three signatures (or an explicit pass).
- **Edge Cases**: Metrics whose subject cannot be improved on demand — probe with a known-bad change and require movement in the opposite direction instead.

## Notes and Observations

The reason this pattern is needed at all is asymmetry of default readings. A metric that swings wildly gets investigated immediately; a metric that never moves gets congratulated. The failure mode with the least natural scrutiny is the one that most needs an active probe.

Its sharpest diagnostic — a correctness fix that lowers the score — is worth remembering separately, because it requires no baseline, no history, and no statistics. One correct change moving the number the wrong way is sufficient on its own.
