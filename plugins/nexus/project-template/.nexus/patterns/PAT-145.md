# PAT-145: A Deferred Ruling Still Ships Its Consequence
*Version: 1.0.0 | Date: 2026-08-25 | Sprint: 110*

## Metadata
- **Type**: principle
- **Status**: active
- **Created**: 2026-08-25

## Problem

A decision is correctly deferred. It genuinely needs work that is out of the current scope — a different owner, a different issue, more information — and the deferral is recorded with a reason. Everything about that act is right.

What goes unexamined is the **state left standing in the meantime**. Deferring *which claim is true* reads as covering the consequence of having no ruling, so contradictory, misleading, or half-corrected state ships with a defensible note attached to it. The note is the problem: it converts an open wound into a closed decision. A later consistency pass finds the note, reads "deferred on purpose", and inherits the verdict without re-examining what the deferral actually covered.

The distinction that gets lost: **deferring a ruling and deferring the reader-facing consequence of not having one are different acts**, and the first is routinely mistaken for the second.

## Context

- **Use When**:
  - Recording a deferral, an out-of-scope ruling, or a "not this issue" routing decision
  - Reviewing a finding that an earlier phase marked deferred-by-design — at Validate, at closure, or in any later consistency pass
  - Closing an issue or a sprint that carries known-unresolved contradictions
  - Two artifacts disagree and the disagreement is about to be recorded as intentional
- **Not When**:
  - The standing state is already correct and merely incomplete — silence is not contradiction
  - The consequence genuinely cannot be mitigated without making the deferred ruling first
  - The state is internal and pre-release, with no consumer who could act on it before the ruling lands
- **Prerequisites**: The deferred item has an identifiable consumer — a reader, a caller, a downstream artifact — whose behaviour depends on the unruled state.

## Solution

At every deferral, write down **two** things instead of one:

1. **What was deferred** — the ruling, its owner, and where it will be made.
2. **What is left standing** — the concrete state that ships until the ruling lands, named as state, not as "the open question".

Then ask a single question of item 2: *is there a remedy that costs nothing against the deferred ruling?* There usually is, and it is usually cheap — designate an authority and point at it, add a qualifier, mark one side provisional, scope a claim to the case it is actually true for. None of these decide the deferred question; all of them stop the consequence from shipping unqualified.

**When re-encountering a deferral in a later phase, treat it as requiring re-examination rather than inheriting its verdict.** The earlier ruling was correct about what it ruled on. It was not a ruling about what could be left standing, and it was made without the evidence the later phase now has.

### Implementation

1. In the deferral record, add a line: `left standing: {concrete state}`.
2. Name the consumer of that state.
3. Propose the cheapest qualifier or pointer that does not touch the deferred ruling.
4. Apply it, or record explicitly that no such remedy exists and why.
5. At any later review that encounters the deferral, re-run steps 2–4 against current evidence before honouring the earlier call.

## Rationale

- **Theoretical Basis**: A deferral is a decision about *ownership and timing*, not about *state*. Treating it as though it settles both is a scope error, and it is a self-concealing one — the recorded reason makes the standing state look adjudicated, so the normal mechanisms that surface contradictions (consistency passes, reviews, later phases) read the note and stop. The remedy asymmetry is what makes this worth a rule: the ruling is expensive and out of scope, while the consequence's mitigation is usually one pointer.
- **Empirical Evidence**: ISS-089 (Sprint 110) shipped exactly this. Two getting-started guides asserted opposite claims about whether the framework runs in the vendor's web app; the disagreement was recorded as deliberate and routed to two distribution issues, with the note "both lines disagree on purpose". The deferral was **correct about which claim is true** — that genuinely required the other issues. It was wrong about what could be left standing: two entry-point guides asserting opposites, with the contradicted claim sitting in the **write-once canonical owner** the whole documentation strategy pointed readers toward. Found at Validate by re-examining a deferred-by-design finding instead of inheriting its verdict. The remedy cost one pointer — designate the installation guide as the host-requirements authority and point at it — and left the factual ruling entirely untouched. It also cost three follow-on edits for one line, which is the ordinary shape of this repair, not an argument against it.
- **Design Decisions**: The rule is stated as "write two things" rather than "audit your deferrals" because the failure happens at *authoring* time, in the same breath as a correct decision. A retrospective audit catches it late and reads as second-guessing a ruling that was right.

## Anti-Patterns

- **Anti-Pattern 1**: Recording "deferred on purpose" as the complete disposition of a finding.
  - **Why Wrong**: It names the ruling and leaves the state unnamed, so the state has no owner and no review trigger.
  - **Example**: Two guides left asserting opposites, with the deferral note standing in for a fix.
- **Anti-Pattern 2**: Inheriting an earlier phase's deferred verdict at review time.
  - **Why Wrong**: The earlier phase ruled with less evidence and on a different question. Re-examination is not re-litigation.
  - **Example**: A consistency pass finding the contradiction, reading the note, and moving on.
- **Anti-Pattern 3**: Waiting for the ruling before qualifying the state.
  - **Why Wrong**: It couples a cheap mitigation to an expensive decision and ships the consequence for however long the ruling takes.
  - **Example**: Leaving a claim unqualified for four sprints because the issue that owns it is unscheduled.
- **Anti-Pattern 4**: Resolving the consequence by silently deciding the deferred question.
  - **Why Wrong**: It smuggles a ruling past its owner, in an artifact whose reviewers believe the question is still open.
  - **Example**: Deleting the contradicted claim outright rather than pointing at the authority — which asserts an answer nobody ruled.

## Consequences
- **Benefits**: Stops correct deferrals from shipping incorrect state · gives the standing consequence an owner and a review trigger · preserves the deferred ruling intact, so the cheap fix does not pre-empt the expensive decision.
- **Tradeoffs**: Every deferral gets a second line and a small decision · the qualifier is visible to consumers, which surfaces uncertainty that was previously invisible — usually a benefit, occasionally noise.
- **Risks**: The qualifier can drift into an answer. Keep it to authority-and-pointer or explicit provisionality; the moment it starts asserting which claim is true, the deferred ruling has been made by the wrong party.

## Examples

### Before/After Comparison

**Without Pattern**: A contradiction between two entry-point documents is found, correctly identified as belonging to a different issue, and recorded as a deliberate deferral. Both documents continue to assert opposite things to new readers, and the note prevents the next three reviews from re-opening it.

**With Pattern**: The same deferral is recorded with a second line naming what ships in the meantime and who reads it. One document is designated the authority for that topic; the other points at it instead of asserting. The factual ruling remains open and owned by the same downstream issue, and no reader sees an unqualified contradiction.

### Application Example

At an evaluation phase, a finding marked "deferred by design" in the implementation log was re-examined rather than inherited. The deferral held on the question it had ruled on. It failed on what it had left standing — a contradicted claim sitting in the canonical owner of that entire content tier. Designating an authority and pointing at it closed the consequence for the cost of one line plus three follow-on edits, and changed nothing about the deferred question.

## Validation
- **Success Indicator**: Each deferral record carries an explicit `left standing:` line, a named consumer, and either an applied qualifier or a recorded reason no qualifier exists.
- **Edge Cases**: When the standing state is a *silence* rather than a contradiction — a topic nobody covers — this pattern does not fire; that is a coverage gap, tracked as work, not as a shipped falsehood.

## Notes and Observations

The strongest version of this pattern is procedural rather than persuasive: the `left standing:` line makes the omission structurally visible, so the discipline does not depend on anyone remembering the distinction under pressure. Without it, the failure recurs precisely because the deferral was *right* — and a right decision is the last place anyone looks for an unhandled consequence.
